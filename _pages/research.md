## Working Papers

**The Effect of Education Reform on the Marriage Market: Evidence from India** (with Sanghmitra Gautam)
{: style="margin-bottom: 0.2em"}

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>
<div markdown="1">

We study the impact of a large-scale school construction program, the District Primary Education Program (DPEP), on female education and marriage market outcomes in India. Using data from the District Level Household and Facility Survey (DLHS) and the Indian Census, we employ an event-study framework that exploits variation in DPEP exposure across districts and birth cohorts. Exposure raised educational attainment at both the extensive and intensive margins, with gains for women roughly twice those for men. These gains carry over to the marriage market: exposed women marry and have a first birth later and marry husbands closer to them in both age and education, and their children receive more prenatal and delivery care. Combining the reduced-form estimates with the marital sorting framework of Choo and Siow (2006), we find higher marital gains in exposed districts among couples with some education but not among couples with none, which suggests that DPEP raised the marital return to education.

</div>
